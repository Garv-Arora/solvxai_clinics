**The short version:** Google says there is no special trick for its AI features. Useful, original content matters most. And there is now one switch in Search Console that decides whether your site can appear in them at all.

## The scale

Google's AI Mode has more than 1 billion monthly users, and AI Overviews and AI Mode are now one experience. ([Alphabet, Q2 2026](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/))

## What Google says works

Its guide to [optimising for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), updated in July 2026, comes down to this:

- **Content that is not a commodity.** Your own numbers, your own cases, your own clinicians. Not a rewrite of what every other site says.
- **The same fundamentals as Search.** Pages that can be crawled, indexed and read.

## What Google says you do not need

- **llms.txt.** Not needed for its AI features.
- **Content "chunking".** Not needed.
- **Special schema for AI.** Not needed.

And one thing it warns against: pages spun out for every variation of a question. Google treats those as scaled content abuse.

## The switch to check today

Since 31 August 2026, every Search Console property has a [generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024). It includes or excludes your site from AI Overviews, AI Mode and generative Discover.

The default is include. Check that nobody has changed it.

## What this means for a clinic

- Do not pay for an "AI schema" package or an llms.txt file as if either were a lever.
- Do put your real numbers on your procedure pages: how many, how long, what it costs, who does it.
- Do make one good page per procedure per location, not forty thin ones.

The rest of the work is off your site. That is covered in [the listings and reviews playbook](/playbooks/listings-and-reviews/).
