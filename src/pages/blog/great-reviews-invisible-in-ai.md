**The short version:** a Los Angeles dental practice with more than 500 five-star Google reviews was the first name AI gave for one service and absent for three others. Reputation and AI visibility are not the same thing.

## The audit

Elevate DDS, a dental marketing firm, [published an AI visibility audit](https://www.elevatedds.com/ai-seo-for-dentists/) of an Invisalign practice in Van Nuys, Los Angeles. The practice is not named and is not a SolvX.AI client. We read the page on 7 October 2026.

What the audit found:

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Google reviews | 500+, rated 5.0 |
| AI visibility score | 22.2% |
| Dental bonding | Ranked first in ChatGPT and Perplexity |
| Cosmetic dentistry | Ranked fifth |
| Dental implants, whitening, extractions | No AI visibility |
| Share of AI citations from the practice's own site | 3.39% |
| Share of AI citations pointing to competitors | 67.8% |

## What it shows

**A strong reputation does not carry across services.** The AI knew this practice for bonding. For implants, in the same city, it named other people.

**The practice's own website was a small part of the answer.** About 3 in every 100 citations. The rest came from other sites, most of them pointing somewhere else.

**The accurate picture was already there.** The AI had the practice's details right 93% of the time. The problem was not wrong information. It was missing coverage.

## The fix follows from the finding

- One page per service, per location, that answers the questions patients ask about it.
- Profiles and lists that mention the service, not only the practice name.
- Reviews that name the procedure.

That is steps two to four of the five in [the clinic playbook](/playbooks/geo-for-clinics/). Step one is running this kind of baseline for your own practice.
