**The short version:** ask an AI the same question twice and you get two different lists. A screenshot showing your practice named, or missing, tells you about one run. You need the count over many.

## How much answers move

- **Under 1%.** The chance that ChatGPT or Google's AI returns the same list of brands twice for the same question. ([SparkToro research, via Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/))
- **74% a week.** The share of domains ChatGPT cites that change from one week to the next. For Google AI Mode it is 56%. For ChatGPT, the typical question had no single source present in all 17 weeks studied. ([SISTRIX](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/ai-citation-drift-how-stable-are-sources-in-ai-search-results/), 82,619 prompts)
- **60% to 10%.** Reddit's share of ChatGPT responses, before and after mid-September 2025. ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/), 230,000+ prompts)

## What stays steady

The lists change. The pool they are drawn from changes much less. In the SparkToro research, the leading brands still appeared in 55% to 77% of runs.

So the useful number is not "am I in this answer". It is "how often am I in the answer".

## How to measure it

1. Pick the ten questions your patients ask.
2. Ask each one ten times in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Perplexity.
3. Count how often each practice is named. That is your mention rate.
4. Note which pages the answers cite.
5. Do it again next month, with the same questions.

One of the [public case studies](/case-studies/wall-street-dermatology-new-york/) on this site reports exactly this kind of number: a dermatology practice that went from being named in 28% of test prompts to 40%.

## Why it matters when you hire someone

If an agency shows you a single screenshot as proof, ask how many times they ran it. If the answer is once, it is an anecdote.
