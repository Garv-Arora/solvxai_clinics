**The short version:** 47% of US patients have used an AI tool to research a healthcare provider, and 36% say it influenced who they chose. The answer they get names three or four practices.

## The numbers

- **47%** of patients have used an AI tool such as ChatGPT to research a provider. Nine months earlier it was 31%. ([rater8, 2026 Patient Choice Report](https://rater8.com/2026-patient-choice-report/), 992 US adults, April 2026)
- **36%** of those who searched for a provider say AI influenced their final choice. (rater8, same report)
- **45%** of US consumers now use AI tools for local business recommendations. A year earlier it was 6%. That makes AI the third most-used source, after Google and Facebook. ([BrightLocal, 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), 1,002 US consumers)
- **43%** of health searches on Google show an AI answer. Across all searches it is 20.5%. ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-triggers/), 146 million results pages)

## What the patient sees

Not ten blue links. A short paragraph and three or four names, each with a reason: a number of procedures, a board certification, a review score.

A practice is in that paragraph or it is not. There is no page two.

## Where the names come from

An AI builds the answer from four kinds of pages:

1. **Your own pages.** Procedure, city and provider pages.
2. **Lists and directories.** RealSelf, Zocdoc, Healthgrades, local "best of" lists.
3. **Patient reviews.** Google, RealSelf, Healthgrades.
4. **Press and mentions.** Local news, society pages, publications.

Three of the four are not your website. That is where most practices have the gap.

## What to do this week

Ask the question your patients ask, ten times, in ChatGPT and in Google's AI Mode. Write down every practice named and every page cited. That list is your work plan.
